## Session: Auto-PA arrival lead — per-stop long-approach bump

Added an automatic per-stop arrival-lead bump to the OCR auto-driver. Long-PA stops (transfer guides, termini) fire their arrival announcement earlier so the guide finishes nearer the platform instead of overrunning.

### Why a flat per-stop bump, not the original "dynamic threshold" plan
TODO had "derive 900 vs 1200 from PA-longer-than-route-average." Walked the audio data first (177 arrival PAs across 14 routes): avg 28.9s, range 7–128s, within-route spread 4–8×. The naive plan — and any z-score/σ model — keys off PA *duration*. But the governing insight is **long PA ↔ slow approach**: Atami's 128s guide sits at a yellow-signal crawl, so in *distance* terms it needs ~the same lead as Shinjuku's 59s. Distance self-compensates for the wildly varying time-budget. A scaled/statistical bump would double-count and fire the slow-approach termini absurdly early (Atami ≈ +5.5σ on duration). So: flat capped bump, identical +400 for every long stop. User drove this — "same for atami and shinjuku."

### The numbers fall out of the lead math, not guesswork
900m covers ~40s of talking at ~23 m/s cruise → threshold = 40s (the duration 900m can't finish). Bump to 1300m covers ~56s ≈ the user's manual Shinjuku setting (~1300m). 40s cutoff catches 35/177 stops (20%), every one a recognizable junction/terminus — no false positives. Derived once from audio headers (`soundfile.info`, header read, no decode), so zero route.json authoring and it applies to future routes.

### Genuine upgrade path (parked, not built)
The causally-correct model is time-based: fire when `distance / cruise_speed ≤ pa_dur`. Zero config, auto-adapts to both PA length and approach speed. Not built — the self-compensation means no current route breaks the long-PA/slow-approach correlation, so the flat bump suffices. Revisit only if a route ever pairs a long PA with a *fast* approach (or vice versa). Logged in the README, not as a TODO (no current trigger).
